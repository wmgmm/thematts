Exercises

Hands-on activities to explore M365 Copilot's capabilities.

Exercise 1 – My First M365 Prompt (The Roast)

Test the Connection: Let's verify Copilot can see your specific data by asking for a "Roast". If it mocks you for that specific 8am meeting next Tuesday, you know it's working.

"For a script: Analyze my actual calendar for the next 7 days with specifics and roast it."

Note: This demonstrates Graph Connection (reading your calendar). The model picker (top right) can stay on Auto; pick Think deeper for a better roast.

Exercise 2 – The "Triage Assistant - Email+Teams"

The Goal: We want to catch items you might have opened on your phone but haven't actually answered. We will swap the "Unread" filter for a "Time" filter to ensure nothing slips through the cracks.

"Review my emails and Teams chats from the last 3 workdays. Ignore newsletters, ads, and automated notifications. Focus on messages from colleagues. Find the top 3 items that have a deadline or a question I haven’t answered yet, ranked by urgency. For each item, show: Message summary Link to the original message Present the results in a simple list."

Why this is better: If you ask for "Unread," Copilot ignores that important email you opened while queuing for coffee. By asking for "Last 3 days" + "Haven't replied," you catch the work you effectively "read and forgot."

Exercise 3 – The "Catch Up" Challenge (Targeted)

The "Pro" Way: In Exercise 2, we did a broad search. Now, let's be precise. Use the / (Slash) to anchor Copilot. Replace [Manager Name] with your actual boss or a close colleague.

"I've been away for a week. Summarize all communication from /[Manager Name] and highlight any direct questions they asked me."

Why use the / ? Typing a name normally is just a fuzzy search (looking for the text). Using / creates a hard link to the specific user profile, ensuring Copilot ignores accidental name matches and looks at the correct source.

💡 Pro Tip: As seen in the menu, the forward slash / isn't just for People. You can anchor to Files, Emails, Meetings, Chats, Sites and SharePoint lists. This forces Copilot to ignore the noise and focus strictly on the data source you select.

Exercise 4 – Word Integrations

Open a blank Word document to complete these tasks.

Exercise 4a: Rough text to meeting agenda

Step 1: Copy and paste this text into your document:

“Team meeting regarding Project Skylark launch date is Friday November 24th at 10am and attendees are Sarah, Mike, and Dave. First we need to review the Q3 bug report which should take 15 mins led by Sarah. Then strictly 30 mins to discuss the marketing budget for the holiday season led by Mike. After that Dave wants to demo the new login feature 15 mins. Finally we need 10 mins for open Q&A. Action items from last time were verify server costs and book the conference room.”

Step 2: Highlight the text in Word, press Alt + i, and use this prompt:

"Convert this text into a professional Meeting Agenda. Create a table for the schedule with columns for Topic, Time, and Owner. Put the Action Items in a separate bulleted list at the bottom."

Exercise 4b: Rough text to Invoice

Step 1: Copy and paste this text into your document:

“Hi team, here are the billable hours for Project Falcon for last month. On Oct 3rd I did 4 hours of Server Maintenance at a rate of £150/hr. Then on the 5th of October, Sarah did 6 hours of UI Design which we bill at £120. On Oct 12, I did another 2 hours of Server Maint. Finally on Oct 20th, Dave did 10 hours of Backend coding at our premium rate of £200/hr. Please process payment net 30. Cheers Steve”

Step 2: Highlight the text in Word, press Alt + i, and use this prompt:

"Convert this text into a formal Invoice Table. Create columns for: Date, Description, Staff Member, Hours, Rate, and Total Cost. Calculate the Total Cost for each row automatically. Format the table: Bold the header row, shade it light blue, and align all money columns to the right. Add a final row summing up the Grand Total."
Exercise 5 – Excel Integration

Open Excel and try these prompts in sequence:

Step 1: Create Data

"Create a table of 50 fictitious employees with columns for Name, Department, Salary, and Hire Date."

Optional: If you struggled to create the sample data above, download the staff.xlsx file instead.

Step 2: Calculate Years of Service

"Add a new column that calculates 'Years of Service' based on the Hire Date and today's date."

Step 3: Add Bonus Column

"Add a column called 'Bonus'. If the Department is Sales, the bonus is 10% of Salary. For everyone else, it is 5%."

Step 4: Generate Emails

"Add a column called 'Email' that combines the first letter of their name and their full last name @company.com, all in lowercase."

Step 5: Department Summary

"Create a new sheet showing the total salary cost breakdown by Department."

Step 6: Highlight Top Earners

"Highlight the top 10% of earners in Red."

Step 7: Data Labels

"Add data labels to the line so I can see the exact number for each year."

Step 8: Bonus – Topic Discovery with the downloaded staff.xlsx file

"For column [Favourite Phrase], create a new column called [Key Theme]. In [Key Theme], extract one funny high-level keyword that represents the main idea of each phrase"
Exercise 6 – Meet Your Specialists (Built-in Agents)

Copilot is a generalist, but these Agents are specialists. Microsoft has built these three dedicated tools to handle complex tasks that standard Copilot might find too heavy. If a Launch button lands on the wrong agent, open the Agents list in Copilot and search by name.

1. Researcher

The Deep Thinker. Unlike standard chat, this agent takes its time. It searches both the Web and your internal files to write a full report with citations.

Launch Researcher

Try: "Research the latest trends in [Industry] and compare them to our internal strategy documents."

2. Analyst

The Data Scientist. Upload a CSV or Excel file, and this agent writes and runs real Python code to clean data, find anomalies, and generate charts.

Optional: If you need a file to play around with try this download the staff.xlsx file instead.

Launch Analyst

Try: Upload a spreadsheet and ask "Visualize the top cost drivers in this data."

3. Prompt Coach

The Teacher. Not sure how to ask? Paste your draft prompt here, and this agent will critique it and rewrite it to be more effective.

Launch Coach

Try: "Help me improve a prompt to summarize a meeting."

Exercise 7 – Choose Your Own Adventure (Explore & Share)

Part A: Exploration. You don't master Copilot by reading a manual; you master it by trying, failing, and tweaking.

  1. Get Inspired: Check the AI Discovery Sprint for general ideas.
  2. Get Templates: Browse Microsoft's official Prompt Library.
  3. The Golden Rule: Pick a prompt, modify it to fit your style, and see what works.

Part B: Share your Experiments. Share what's going well and what's less well, that empowers shared learning

More M365 Copilot Examples

Advanced prompts that combine multiple M365 data sources.

Email Triage (Past 7 Days)

A daily starter: turns a week of inbox noise into three clear piles, with anything urgent or schedulable flagged.

"Review all emails from the past 7 days. Sort into Critical, Action Needed, and Can Wait. Highlight anything that needs scheduling or immediate attention."
Find a Meeting Time

Uses the / anchor so Copilot checks the real calendars of the people you pick, not just yours.

"Suggest a few time options for a meeting with /[Person]."
Task Lists (Daily & Project)

Two flavours: a personal daily list ranked by urgency and importance, and a project list ordered by deadlines and dependencies.

Daily

"Create a daily task list prioritising urgent and important items."

Project

"Create a prioritised task list for [project] based on deadlines and dependencies."
Client Relationship Recap

Pulls every touchpoint with a client into one briefing, so you walk into the next meeting knowing what was promised and what is still open.

"Provide a detailed summary of the recent interactions we've had with [client name], including dates, participants, topics discussed and outcomes. Highlight any commitments made or pending issues that need to be addressed in the upcoming meeting."
Conflict Resolution

Uses semantic search to find conflict (not just keywords), saving you hours of cross-referencing threads.

"Search my recent Teams chats and emails with [Person A] and [Person B] from the last 2 weeks. List every instance where they gave conflicting instructions on [Project Name] and summarize the discrepancy in a table."
Audit Trail

Acts as a forensic tool, finding the exact metadata of a past action to use as evidence in a new action.

"I need to prove we sent the deliverables. Find the specific email where I sent the final [Project] PDF to [Client Name] last month, and draft a follow-up email that references the date and time of that original message."
Meeting Prep

Filters specifically for incoming attachments from a specific person, ignoring your own internal noise.

"I have a meeting with [Customer/Stakeholder] tomorrow. Surface the last 3 files they shared with me via email or Teams, and summarize the 'Next Steps' slide or section from each."
Commitment Tracking

Connects two data points: the promise made in a transcript and the lack of a confirmation signal in the chat.

"Scan all meeting transcripts from [Project Alpha] series in the last month. List every action item assigned to me that does not yet have a corresponding 'Done' or 'Completed' status update in the chat."
Devil's Advocate (Your critical friend)

Copilot argues against the ideas you have been pushing, using only objections and risks that people have actually raised. Turn web search off first (Settings → Personalisation → Advanced) so the counterarguments come from your own data.

"Act as a constructive devil's advocate. Review my emails, meeting recaps, chats, and shared documents from the last three months. Identify the ideas and requests I have been pushing. For each one, build the strongest evidence-based counterargument using concerns, risks, unanswered questions, competing priorities, and previous objections found in the source material. Focus on what has been stated, not inferred. Be specific, challenging, and constructive. End with three changes I should make before raising these ideas again."
One-to-One Prep

From a Cardiff colleague. Run it before the meeting and walk in knowing what is still open between you.

"I have a one-to-one with /[Person] this morning. Identify the key issues we need to discuss and any outstanding email or chat threads between us that still need a reply."
Partnership Evidence Pack

From a Cardiff colleague, who says it turns several hours of admin into one request: disparate evidence across emails, meetings, files and chats becomes one coherent summary.

"Search my emails, meetings, files and chats for evidence related to [partnership or programme] during [date range]. Group the findings into achievements, opportunities, risks, stakeholder engagement, funding activity and actions. Identify gaps and produce an executive-ready summary."
Rewrite for the Audience

From a Cardiff colleague. Works on a pasted draft in Chat, or on selected text in Word. Different versions for different readers, without losing your own voice.

"Rewrite this for [audience]. Keep my voice. Make it less corporate and soften anything that could read as criticism."
Pricing History

Turns unstructured email history into structured data (a price trend analysis) without you opening a single PDF.

"Check my email history for quotes received from [Vendor X] over the last two years. Extract the total price and line-item cost for [Service Y] from each email and plot the price increase trend."
Root Cause (Excel)

Edit with Copilot runs Python behind the scenes, so it can do clustering analysis that standard Excel formulas cannot.

"Analyze this xlsx file. Use Python to cluster this date [ADD SOME SIMPLE CONTEXT]"
Slide Mirroring (PowerPoint)

Solves the biggest pain point of AI slides (bad formatting) by forcing it to "clone" the style of an existing, approved slide.

"Create a new slide for 'Q4 Strategy' that mirrors the exact layout, font size, and visual style of slide 3 in /[Q3_Review.pptx], but populate it with the bullet points from /[Q4_Memo.docx]."
Knowledge Transfer (Teams)

Uses "Graph Signal" (activity/popularity) to determine what is "important" rather than just giving you a list of everything.

"I am onboarding a New Hire to [Project X]. Create a 'Read Me' list by finding the 5 most active documents and the 3 most important decision threads from our team channel in the last 6 months."
Calendar & Capacity Audit

An agentic workflow: Copilot audits your week with Work IQ, checks meeting attendees against the org hierarchy, and proposes a plan to reclaim focus time. Nothing is changed until you approve the plan.

"Execute a comprehensive audit of my Outlook calendar for the upcoming week to optimize my capacity. First, leverage Work IQ to generate an analytical summary detailing the total hours committed to synchronous meetings, identify the days with the highest meeting density, and locate any existing uninterrupted focus blocks of two hours or more. Before making any modifications to my schedule, ask me to define my top three strategic objectives for the week and outline any personal time boundaries I must protect. Additionally, analyze the attendee lists of all my scheduled meetings against our Microsoft Entra ID organizational hierarchy to determine which recurring syncs or peer-level meetings are the lowest risk to reschedule. Once I provide my constraints, present a proposed execution plan that categorizes meetings to accept, recurring meetings to decline or shift to asynchronous Teams updates, and proposes new protected focus blocks. Do not decline any meetings, alter any calendar events, or send cancellation emails until I explicitly approve the specific execution plan."
Meeting Recap Deck

Chains Teams transcripts, shared files and PowerPoint generation into one workflow: a week of project meetings becomes an executive deck, checked with you before it builds.

"Create a comprehensive executive PowerPoint deck summarizing the key outcomes, strategic decisions, and identified risks from all project-related meetings I attended over the last seven days. Access the Work IQ semantic index to pull the relevant meeting transcripts from Teams and retrieve any supporting PDF or Excel files that were shared within those specific meeting chats. Before building the presentation, present a list of the meetings you intend to include and ask me to define the intended audience (e.g., executive leadership, internal engineering team, or external client) so you can accurately adjust the required level of detail. Once I approve the meeting list and audience persona, build a PowerPoint deck utilizing our corporate SharePoint master template. Structure the deck to include a title slide with the date range, one summary slide per major workstream grouping related meetings, a dedicated slide for 'Key Decisions Made' with assigned personnel owners, and a final 'Open Items & Next Steps' slide mapping due dates. Save the finalized deck to my OneDrive in the '/Presentations/Recaps/' folder and send me a direct Teams notification link when the task is complete."
Work IQ On or Off

Copilot Chat is now one chat. The Work IQ button (top left) decides whether it can see your work data. It is on by default.

🛡️
Work IQ On

Scope: Your work data (Emails, Teams, OneDrive, SharePoint), plus the web if web search is on.

Privacy: Total. Your data never leaves the tenant and is never used to train models.

"Catch me up on the Alpha Project email thread."
🌐
Work IQ Off

Scope: The public internet and general knowledge only. Nothing from your inbox or files.

Privacy: Total. Your data never leaves the tenant and is never used to train models.

"What are the top AI trends for 2026?"
💡
The Web Search Toggle

Under Settings → Personalisation → Advanced. Switch it off when you want an answer grounded in your work data only, with no outside context mixed in.
