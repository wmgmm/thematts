WHAT SHOULD THE INFOGRAPHIC EXPLAIN?
Give me whatever you have to work with. You can:
• describe a subject or idea
• give me some notes or background
• upload a document
• upload a spreadsheet
Optional details:
[ADD WHAT TO FOCUS ON, OR LEAVE BLANK]
WHAT TO DO
Use your best judgement to work out what matters most from the information provided.
If useful, search the web to understand the subject, check facts or add helpful context.
Turn the information into a clear visual story.
• Focus on the 3–5 ideas that best explain the subject.
• Don't try to include everything.
• Explain through pictures wherever possible.
• Use visual metaphors, people, objects and simple scenes.
• Keep words to a minimum.
• Use short, conversational headings.
• Give the most important idea the strongest visual emphasis.
• Create a clear visual journey across the page.
• Leave plenty of white space.
• Remove anything that doesn't help explain the subject.
Someone should be able to glance at the infographic and understand the main idea before reading the detail.
VISUAL STYLE
Create the infographic in a hand-drawn visual-facilitation / sketchnote style.
Use:
• warm white paper
• loose black marker outlines
• natural hand lettering
• muted red, mustard yellow, dusty blue and soft grey
• simple, friendly editorial illustrations
• arrows and lines where they help explain connections
• strong visual metaphors that make complex ideas easier to understand
Make it feel like a skilled visual facilitator has taken something complicated and explained it clearly on one sheet of paper.
Keep it human, spacious and slightly imperfect.
Avoid dense layouts, tiny text, excessive speech bubbles, decorative clutter, repeated ideas and polished corporate diagrams.
Above all: explain visually, don't just decorate the words.
