How this is scored

A teaching model, not a measurement. The live run in Exercise 07 is the measurement. The rules are fixed and simple so you can argue with them.

  • Speed starts at 100. A human costs 10 at the matrix, 45 at generating, 20 at evaluating and 10 at review. An AI costs 5 per stage. Nobody costs nothing.
  • Errors caught starts at 55 for an AI-written question or 70 for a human-written one, then adds 15 for a human-read matrix, 5 for an AI matrix, 25 for a human evaluation, 12 for an AI evaluation (format and open-endedness, not fairness in effect) and 5 for human review before the bank. If AI generated and AI evaluated and the only human is the review with no one at the matrix, that final 5 is lost: the person skims because two agents already passed it.
  • Human effort counts 1 for the matrix, 4 for writing, 2 for evaluating, 1 for review, out of 8. Six or more is flagged as fatigue.
  • Accountability is Low if a human reviews before the bank, Medium if a human evaluated or wrote but nobody reviewed, otherwise High.
  • Data and legal starts high, because the CV is personal data. It drops a level if a person reads the question before a candidate does and another if a person read the matrix, and is High whenever AI wrote it and no person reads it.
  • Security is Low with no AI stage. Otherwise it starts at how exposed the CV route is to a planted line, and drops a level for a human read before the bank and for a human at the matrix.
  • Quality follows the errors-caught score: under 50 High, 50 to 75 Medium, above 75 Low, one level worse because a question asked cannot be unasked.
  • People is Medium when most of the work sits with people (fatigue), when a human both checks and signs off, or on the rubber-stamp flag, otherwise Low. Nothing here is High: people problems show up as slowness and skipped checks, which the other rows then catch.
  • Acceptable or too risky: too risky if any row is High, or if more than one row is Medium. Hiring earns the stricter line.
